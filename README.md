# AI Policy Influence Map

A working contact and research map of the people and organizations shaping U.S. AI regulation: members of Congress, the executive branch, state officials, foreign governments, super PACs and their donors, industry and investors, academics and think tanks, media voices, advocacy groups, international AI leaders, and small-business AI voices.

It was built to support outreach for an event, so every entry is organized around one question: who is this, where do they stand, and how do you reach them through official channels?

> **Status:** v5, research snapshot as of November 1, 2026. AI policy moves weekly. Treat everything here as a starting point and verify before use.

## Background: the two camps

The fight over U.S. AI policy has largely split into two camps:

- **"One national standard" / light-touch.** Backed by much of the industry and the White House. Favors federal preemption of state AI laws and argues a 50-state patchwork would hurt U.S. competitiveness with China.
- **"Guardrails."** Wants safety rules and wants states to keep the ability to regulate. Argues preemption without strong federal rules would leave the public unprotected.

**The money.** The largest AI-focused super PAC network is **Leading the Future** (pro-industry), which operates through party-aligned affiliates (Think Big for Democrats, American Mission for Republicans) and is linked to Build American AI, a 501(c)(4) that does not disclose donors. **Public First**, co-founded by former members of Congress Brad Carson and Chris Stewart, was created to counter it. Meta also runs its own PACs.

**Where it stands.** No federal AI framework has passed. Preemption remains the central dispute, and little is expected to move before the November 3, 2026 midterms.

## Data quality: read before using

- **Green text** in the source column means the contact was confirmed on the organization's own page during research (most marked `Verified Nov 1 2026 — <source URL>`). **Red text** means not yet confirmed.
- **159 rows** have a verified contact. About **280 rows** carry a Notes entry saying they weren't reached in the November 1 research pass (it hit a search limit). Those are queued for the next pass.
- **Senate** phones come from the official senate.gov list. **House** DC phones and office rooms were confirmed against house.gov for all 19 original House members on November 1.
- **New rows added November 1** say "New. Source: …" in Notes. Rows marked **"VERIFY current role"** were added from background knowledge and haven't been checked on a page.
- **Role changes** found during research (e.g. Yann LeCun now at AMI Labs, Main Street America's new CEO, Manus acquired by Meta) are recorded in each row's Notes. The title columns haven't been changed, so check Notes first.
- **Bill numbers** mostly still need to be confirmed on [congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov).

## Privacy

Entries use **official, company, institutional, government or speaker-bureau contact channels only**. Emails are recorded only when they appear on an official page (press and role inboxes, contacts named on faculty or team pages, press-release media contacts, FEC filings), never guessed from a pattern. The workbook deliberately excludes home addresses, personal phone numbers, private emails, data-broker sources, and contact details for private-individual donors. Please keep it that way when adding entries.
