# AI Policy Influence Map

A working contact and research map of the people and organizations shaping U.S. AI regulation: members of Congress, the executive branch, state officials, foreign governments, super PACs and lobbying groups, industry and investors, academics and think tanks, media voices, and advocacy groups.

It was built to support outreach for an event, so every entry is organized around one question: who is this, where do they stand, and how do you reach them through official channels?

> **Status:** research snapshot as of late September 2026. AI policy moves weekly. Treat everything here as a starting point and verify before use.

## Background: the two camps

The fight over U.S. AI policy has largely split into two camps:

- **"One national standard" / light-touch.** Backed by much of the industry and the White House. Favors federal preemption of state AI laws and argues a 50-state patchwork would hurt U.S. competitiveness with China.
- **"Guardrails."** Wants safety rules and wants states to keep the ability to regulate. Argues preemption without strong federal rules would leave the public unprotected.

**The money.** The largest AI-focused super PAC network is **Leading the Future** (pro-industry), which operates through party-aligned affiliates (Think Big for Democrats, American Mission for Republicans) and is linked to Build American AI, a 501(c)(4) that does not disclose donors. **Public First**, co-founded by former members of Congress Brad Carson and Chris Stewart, was created to counter it. Meta also runs its own PACs.

**Where it stands.** No federal AI framework has passed. Preemption remains the central dispute, and with the November 3, 2026 midterms approaching, little is expected to move before the election. The Cruz–Thune–Klobuchar bill is the one to watch.

**Politics are shifting.** AI has become a liability on both sides, driven in part by voter anger over data centers and electricity costs.

## Data quality: read before using

- **Senate contacts** come from the official senate.gov list (updated September 23, 2026) and are reliable.
- **House contacts** were only confirmed for a few offices. Unconfirmed entries are shown in **red text**, meaning "check before using." Use [clerk.house.gov](https://clerk.house.gov) for the official member list.
- **Bill numbers** mostly still need to be confirmed on [congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov).
- **Fields marked "verify"** (names, titles) should be checked before outreach.
- **Staff names and emails are blank on purpose.** Congressional staff turn over often, and a wrong name hurts an invitation. Use each office's scheduling or event-request form to reach the scheduler.

## Privacy

Entries use **official, company, or institutional contact channels only**. The workbook deliberately excludes home addresses, personal phone numbers, private emails, and names of ordinary donors or employees pulled from FEC filings. Please keep it that way when adding entries.
